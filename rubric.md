# Apprentice Evaluation Rubric

The canonical reference for scoring apprentices. Six competencies, each with a Zone 1-5 progression from foundational to advanced. Zones are cumulative in spirit: someone at Zone 3 generally still meets the lower zones.

Use this at **review time** to place an apprentice on each ladder based on accumulated evidence. For fast in-the-moment logging, use `capture-format.md` instead, which strips these down to a one-line tag per competency.

The six competencies:

1. Technical Knowledge and Skills
2. Attention to Detail
3. Problem Solving, Creativity, and Debugging
4. Initiative to Improve and Learn
5. Productivity and Use of Time
6. Communication and Teamwork

---

## 1. Technical Knowledge and Skills

**Zone 1**
- Can independently complete straightforward tasks.
- Has a fundamental understanding of their primary language and framework.

**Zone 2**
- Identifies common programming patterns within their language.
- Uses their framework effectively but has limited grasp of underlying concepts.

**Zone 3**
- Independently handles moderately complex tasks.
- Comprehensive understanding of their framework.
- Can independently build a simple CRUD app, showing a solid grasp of common concepts and patterns.
- Occasionally mentors and supports colleagues.
- Researches and adopts new tools and libraries relevant to their framework.
- Completes most assigned tasks independently.

**Zone 4**
- Understands basic CS concepts, including API interactions and dev ops.
- Can build apps with auth, protected routes, dynamic routing, and common data structures.
- Regularly mentors peers, considered a reliable resource.
- Recognizes common patterns across multiple languages.
- Can scope and plan projects effectively.

**Zone 5**
- Explores and integrates additional libraries and frameworks to sharpen architectural assessments.
- Explains advanced concepts clearly to other apprentices.
- Designs and architects solutions to complex problems with real depth.

---

## 2. Attention to Detail

**Zone 1**
- In PRs, explains how to best test their code.
- Generally consistent line spacing, indentation, and code cleanliness.
- Generally well-detailed commit messages.

**Zone 2**
- Git branches: usually resolves Git problems. Doesn't often push to the wrong branch, get stuck on merges, etc.
- Code consistently meets minimum acceptance criteria.
- PRs: good at incorporating feedback and pushing up updates.
- Consistently documents and comments their code.

**Zone 3**
- Follows the project's coding conventions (hooks/styles/etc. go where the project stores them).
- Peer review: gives feedback on teammates' PRs on syntax, file-naming, simple backend calls.
- Commit messages: detail explaining what changes took place.
- Rarely leaves `console.log`s or stray code behind when committing or opening PRs.

**Zone 4**
- Generally looks out for edge cases (null checks, etc.).
- Considers the impact of edge cases and plans for them: failing gracefully, handling an empty array, etc.
- Reviews PRs more holistically than syntax: checks efficiency, alignment with requirements and best practices.

**Zone 5**
- Mentorship: leaves PR feedback that helps teammates grow.

---

## 3. Problem Solving, Creativity, and Debugging

**Zone 1**
- Independently handles simple tasks (CRUD), improving at initiating unfamiliar work.
- Takes a straightforward approach to simple problems, refining strategies to avoid overcomplicating.
- Developing the habit of seeking help in a timely way.
- Handles minimal debugging but needs significant assistance.

**Zone 2**
- Needs moderate help debugging, showing progress.
- Routinely asks for help within a reasonable timeframe.
- Can break tasks into manageable parts.

**Zone 3**
- Skilled at writing detailed tickets and acceptance criteria.
- Needs little help debugging, strong independent problem-solving.
- Growing confidence tackling unfamiliar tasks.

**Zone 4**
- Strong at reading and researching third-party docs.
- Regularly helps other apprentices get past blockers.
- Robust debugging and troubleshooting.

**Zone 5**
- Balances thorough solutions against the practicality of quicker approaches.
- Addresses and resolves complex problems.

---

## 4. Initiative to Improve and Learn

**Zone 1**
- Generally completes clearly-defined tasks.

**Zone 2**
- Asks questions to make sure they understand concepts.

**Zone 3**
- Incorporates feedback: learns from mistakes and doesn't often repeat them.
- Research: actively tries to learn about a problem to solve it.

**Zone 4**
- Incorporates feedback: quickly folds it into their code, doesn't often repeat mistakes.

**Zone 5**
- Proactive research: researches tools, libraries, and stacks outside the main framework, makes sound decisions on what to include. Works with less clarity in requirements by seeking out details.
- Team lead: defines and assigns tasks to others, shares helpful tech with the group.

---

## 5. Productivity and Use of Time

**Zone 1**
- Increasingly works independently, fewer check-ins needed from leads or managers.
- Regularly meets deadlines, often completes tickets within the original estimate.
- Frequently on time to meetings.

**Zone 2**
- Works effectively within expected timeframes for tickets and reviews, increasingly autonomous.
- Improving punctuality, on time to meetings more consistently.
- More independent, less frequent supervision needed.

**Zone 3**
- Understands and applies project workflows, contributes efficiently to process.
- Consistently punctual to meetings.
- Routinely completes tasks within expected timeframes, strong autonomy.
- Works independently most of the time without check-ins.

**Zone 4**
- Deep understanding of agile, implements it effectively.
- Attends all meetings, actively participates or leads, may serve as scrum facilitator or coordinator.

**Zone 5**
- Juggles multiple tasks or projects, switches context without losing focus.
- Comprehensive understanding of the overall project and how efforts ladder up to goals.
- Reliably delivers on time and helps teammates hit their deadlines.
- Proactively communicates about meeting cadence and agendas, often takes on facilitator/coordinator roles.

---

## 6. Communication and Teamwork

**Zone 1**
- Direct communication: responds to DMs and joins project channel discussions regularly.
- Active meeting participation: contributes relevant input.
- Feedback integration: begins to fold in feedback from teammates or leads.

**Zone 2**
- Proactive async communication: engages in team discussions when prompted, asks clarifying questions.
- Feedback integration: actively seeks and integrates feedback, shows understanding and application of suggestions.

**Zone 3**
- Independent communication: contributes to group discussions without prompting, brings unique ideas, engages respectfully.
- Feedback and peer mentoring: consistently uses feedback to improve work, occasionally guides peers.

**Zone 4**
- Cross-departmental communication: communicates complex ideas to devs and other departments, contributes valuable documentation like READMEs.
- Feedback reception and construction: gives constructive feedback while staying receptive and productive to feedback from all sources.
- Works productively with all teammates and stakeholders, consistently acts on feedback.

**Zone 5**
- Comprehensive stakeholder engagement: proactively communicates implications of work, liaises with other departments, tailors communication to the audience.
- Documentation: diagrams complex concepts, leads doc creation, works with multiple inputs and stakeholders.
- Actively mentors others on their team(s).
